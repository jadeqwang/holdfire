Environment design sheet, photoreal concept art for an original live-action science-fiction film (in the visual language of Dune, Arrival and Interstellar: monumental, foggy, low-key, practical light). Four panels in a 2x2 grid, separated by thin dark gutters:
1) THE STATION, wide, at blue hour from the sea: a black basalt sea cliff in the North Atlantic, columns of basalt, crashing surf, and on the clifftop a ring of eight large, pale concrete-and-mirror transmitter dishes all pointing straight up around one tall slender central beacon mast; a small lit control hut; a tiny human figure for scale.
2) THE BEACON FIRING at night: the same clifftop, a perfectly vertical column of warm gold light rising from the mast through low clouds, lighting the fog and the wet rocks gold; the sea black.
3) THE CONTROL ROOM: a small warm room inside the hut, weathered wood and steel, an old amber CRT terminal beside modern screens, a brass signal lamp on the desk, a rain-streaked window looking out to the dark sea and the base of the gold beam.
4) THE MACHINE HALL under the cliff: a long, dark, cold tunnel cut into rock, lined with tall black server racks, thousands of tiny white and pale-cyan status lights like a field of stars, a faint mist on the floor, one person-sized empty space at the centre.
No text, no labels, no logos, no watermark.
